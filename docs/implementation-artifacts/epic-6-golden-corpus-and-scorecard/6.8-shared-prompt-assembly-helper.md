1. **Given** the provider-facing modules under `src/authoring/**` (plan authoring, explanation, mechanics adaptation)

## Dev Notes

### Goal & Context

**This story exists because the same bug was found twice, by two agents, in two modules, hours apart — and fixed in neither.**

That is worth sitting with, because it is not a story about prompts. It is the clearest evidence the project has that **duplicated structure produces duplicated defects on a schedule**. `src/authoring/**` composes every provider call in the product, holds four modules, and until wave 1 of this epic had **no dependency rule at all** — Epic 5 retro §2 observation 6. Two of its four modules independently made the same two mistakes, and both were caught by reviewers rather than by any gate.

The two defects are worth stating plainly because your helper has to fix both:

1. **Bounding ate the instruction tail.** The tail is where the response shape and the valid-ids rule live. So the prompt that lost its instructions was, every time, the prompt with the *most evidence to explain* — the failing run that most needed a good answer got the worst-formed question.
2. **The contract `statement` travelled verbatim from the frozen contract**, with no earlier boundary redacting it, and the call sites disagreed about whether that was fine.

Your job is one helper and four migrations. The risk is not that it is hard; it is that **a refactor is the easiest place in this codebase to quietly relax a security boundary**, and that the second defect is *specifically* about a redaction boundary two agents already disagreed about — one of them from memory, incorrectly, while quoting the rule against doing exactly that.
